# Generic idea antipatterns and the question that rescues each

Judges develop an allergy to certain phrases and shapes because they hear them at
every event and they reliably signal a team that has not thought past the first idea.
Each antipattern below is listed with the phrase or shape that triggers it, why judges
discount it, and the single question whose answer turns it into a specific idea. Do
not produce an idea matching one of these shapes unless the rescue question has been
answered in the idea itself.

## "An AI-powered platform for X"

Why it fails: "platform" means the team has not chosen a user or a flow, and
"AI-powered" describes the tool rather than the benefit. Judges hear scope without a
demo. Rescue question: what is the one thing a named person does on it in the first
ninety seconds, and what does the screen show when it works?

## "A chatbot that answers questions about X"

Why it fails: the shape is a text box over a prompt, indistinguishable from the other
five in the room, and the demo is someone typing. Rescue question: what artefact does
it change, or what action does it take, without anyone typing a question?

## "A dashboard to visualise X"

Why it fails: display without decision. Judges cannot see any insight the team had,
only that a charting library works. Rescue question: which single number, when it
crosses which threshold, triggers which action for which role?

## "Uber for X" and other formula clones

Why it fails: the formula signals borrowed thinking. Judges assume the team has not
asked what is different about X. Rescue question: which assumption of the original
product is false for X's users, and what does the product look like once that
assumption is removed?

## "Helps people be more productive / healthier / safer"

Why it fails: no named user, no named pain, no measurable before and after. Impact
scores at the floor. Rescue question: who exactly, in what situation, loses what
today, and what does the demo show them keeping?

## "Uses blockchain / the metaverse / a new model for X"

Why it fails: technology in search of a problem. Judges ask why this needs the
technology and the answer is usually that it does not. Rescue question: what breaks in
the demo if the technology is removed? If nothing breaks, remove it.

## "A social network for X"

Why it fails: value depends on users the team cannot show, so the demo is an empty
feed. Rescue question: what does the product do for the first user, alone, on the
first day, with only public data?

## "A marketplace connecting X with Y"

Why it fails: two empty sides and a signup form; liquidity is the whole problem and it
cannot be demonstrated. Rescue question: which one transaction, between which two
specific people, can be completed end to end in the demo with one side seeded from
real public data?

## "Personalised recommendations for X"

Why it fails: cold start. The judge has no history, so the recommendation is random or
canned. Rescue question: what visible context, available right now without a profile,
drives the recommendation, and can the judge change it and see the result change?

## "We built a classifier with high accuracy"

Why it fails: an unverifiable number and a file-upload demo. Rescue question: when the
classifier fires on one input the judge picks, what happens next in the workflow, and
what happens when it is unsure?

## "An agent that automates your tasks"

Why it fails: unbounded scope, long spinners, agents that never finish. Rescue
question: which three to five tools, on which single workflow, and which one tool call
visibly changes something outside the chat?

## "Summarises documents"

Why it fails: summarisation is a feature of every model product; there is nothing to
demo except shorter text. Rescue question: what decision does the reader make with the
summary, and can the product make or pre-fill that decision instead?

## "A mobile app that lets users…"

Why it fails: "users" is not a person, and "lets" describes permission rather than
benefit. Rescue question: replace "users" with a role in a situation and "lets" with
the verb the product performs on their behalf.

## "Track your X"

Why it fails: manual data entry that nobody does after day two, and a demo with an
empty log. Rescue question: where does the data come from without the user typing it,
and what does the product do the first time the tracked thing changes?

## "We integrated with three APIs"

Why it fails: integration count is not value, and judges have learned that claimed
integrations often do not work live. Rescue question: which one integration is the
demo moment, and can it be shown pulling live data in front of the judge?

## Vision-first pitches

Why it fails: "in the future this could…" fills the time the demo should have used.
Rescue question: what works right now, on a public URL, that a judge can open on their
phone?

## The shape that survives

Ideas that avoid every antipattern share a shape: a named role in a named situation, a
single artefact or action that visibly changes on screen, real data from a source that
needs no account, a closest existing product named openly, and a demo moment written
as one sentence. When an idea is generated in one of the shapes above, apply the
rescue question, rewrite the idea with the answer inside it, and state the twist
explicitly so the judge does not have to infer it.
